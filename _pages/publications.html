--- layout: single title: "About Me" permalink: /about/ author_profile: true --- ## Who I am I'm **Chenchen Ding**, a Master’s student in the **EOI** program at the **Nelson Institute**, University of Wisconsin–Madison (Expected graduation: August 2026). My work sits at the intersection of **remote sensing**, **geospatial data science**, and **environmental monitoring**. Before joining UW–Madison, I completed a B.Eng. and M.Eng. in Petroleum Engineering at the **China University of Petroleum (Beijing)**, working on machine learning models for natural gas forecasting. --- ## Academic background At UW–Madison, the EOI program allows me to combine: - satellite data (MODIS, Landsat, Sentinel-1/2, GEDI, CYGNSS) - deep learning and statistical modeling - environmental applications in vegetation, disturbance, and water --- ## Current Research ### **Surface Water & Wetland Dynamics from Multisensor Observations** I work on remote sensing of surface water using **CYGNSS GNSS-R**, **Sentinel-1 SAR**, **MODIS**, and **DEM**. My research focuses on: - estimating sub-pixel water fractions - building daily global water-body time series (2018–2025) - scalable, reproducible HPC workflows ### **Machine Learning Marathon 2025 — Biodiversity Image Clustering** I also work on: - contrastive-learning-based representation learning - large-scale clustering for biodiversity datasets --- ## How I work I enjoy working with: - large raster datasets - HPC / cloud environments - tiling pipelines and efficient data engineering - ML workflows and model explainability (Permutation, SHAP) --- ## Previous Research and Industry Experience ### **Oil & Gas Sector (China National Aviation Fuel Group, Sinopec, PipeChina)** Previously, I worked on: - emergency response systems for oil pipelines - reliability analysis for natural gas pipeline networks - LSTM-based daily gas demand forecasting These experiences strengthened my skills in data analysis, modeling, and system design. ---
