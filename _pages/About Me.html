Who I am

I'm Chenchen Ding, a Master’s student in the Environmental Observation & Informatics (EOI) program at the Nelson Institute, University of Wisconsin–Madison (Expected graduation: August 2026). My work sits at the intersection of remote sensing, geospatial data science, and environmental monitoring.

Before joining UW–Madison, I completed a B.Eng. and M.Eng. in Petroleum Engineering at the China University of Petroleum (Beijing), where I focused on machine learning models for natural gas forecasting.

Academic background

At UW–Madison, the EOI program allows me to combine:

  • satellite data (MODIS, Landsat, Sentinel-1/2, GEDI, CYGNSS)
  • deep learning and statistical modeling
  • environmental applications in vegetation, disturbance, and water

Current Research

Surface Water & Wetland Dynamics from Multisensor Observations

I work on remote sensing of surface water using CYGNSS GNSS-R, Sentinel-1 SAR, MODIS, and DEM. My research focuses on:

  • estimating sub-pixel water fractions
  • building daily global water-body time series (2018–2025)
  • scalable, reproducible HPC workflows

Machine Learning Marathon 2025 — Biodiversity Image Clustering

  • contrastive-learning–based representation learning
  • large-scale clustering for biodiversity datasets
